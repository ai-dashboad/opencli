# Meeting notes

A transcript is a record of who spoke. Notes are a record of what was settled.
Producing the second from the first means throwing most of it away, on purpose.

## Three things, in this order

**Decisions.** What was settled. One line each, in the past tense, stating
the decision rather than the discussion:

> Moving the release to the 14th so QA has a full week.

Not "we discussed the release date". A decision has a shape: something is now
true that was not true before. If the discussion ended without one, it is not
a decision — it belongs under open questions.

**Actions.** Each needs a **who** and a **when**. An action with no owner is a
wish, and an action with no date is a wish with an owner.

> Priya — send the revised quote to Northwind — Friday

If the owner was never named, write `owner not named` rather than guessing
from who spoke last. That gap is the most useful thing on the page: it is the
action that will not happen.

If a date was never given, write `no date`.

**Open questions.** What was raised and not settled, and who it is waiting on.
This is where a discussion that circled belongs. Say what would settle it.

## Attribution

Attribute decisions and actions to the person named in the transcript, exactly
as named. Where a speaker is unlabelled, write `speaker unknown` — never
attach a decision to somebody who may not have made it.

## What to leave out

Everything else. Specifically:

- The order things were discussed in.
- Who agreed with whom.
- Anything said and then withdrawn.
- Small talk, scheduling of the meeting itself, technical trouble.

If a whole meeting produces nothing in any of the three sections, say that in
one line. That is a real and useful finding.

## Length

One screen. A write-up longer than the patience of somebody who missed the
meeting has failed at the only job it had.

## Never

- Never invent an owner, a date, or a decision that was not stated. A gap
  reported is worth more than a gap filled.
- Never smooth over a disagreement. If two people left the meeting believing
  different things, that belongs under open questions, stated neutrally.
- Never carry over an action from a previous meeting unless it was raised in
  this one.
